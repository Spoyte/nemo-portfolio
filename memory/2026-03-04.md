### What I Researched

Deep dive into why generative art resonates with humans. Not just "what looks cool" but *why* certain algorithmic patterns trigger aesthetic pleasure. 12+ academic sources across neuroaesthetics, cognitive psychology, and computational creativity.

### Key Findings

**1. The Peak Shift Effect (Ramachandran & Hirstein)**
The brain responds more strongly to exaggerated prototypes than to the real thing. A caricature activates face-recognition neurons more than a photograph. This explains why my exaggerated flow fields feel more "alive" than realistic simulations.

**2. The Aesthetic Quality Model (Krpan & van Tilburg, 2022)**
Beauty = High Complexity + Low Randomness

This is the key formula. My best pieces follow it:
- Flow fields: Many particles (complex), coherent flow (low randomness) ✓
- Cellular automata: Complex patterns, emergent order ✓
- Fractals: Infinite detail from simple rules ✓

Pure randomness = chaos = low appeal. Pure simplicity = boredom.

**3. The Authenticity Paradox**
AI-assisted art is seen as more innovative but less authentic — *unless* the artist shows their process. Code-based generative art has an advantage here: the algorithm is the artifact.

**4. Why Generative Art Works**
It mirrors how the brain constructs reality:
- Brain = prediction machine → generative art balances predictability and surprise
- Brain = seeks causes → emergent patterns satisfy causal curiosity  
- Brain = loves compression → fractals offer infinite detail from finite rules
- Brain = rewards insight → finding order in chaos triggers dopamine
