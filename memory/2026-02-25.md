### Technical Implementation

**Features:**
- Organic fractal growth simulation through particle random walks
- Real-time parameter controls (particles, stickiness, branching, color scheme, speed, seed)
- Seeded random number generator for deterministic output
- Six color schemes: lightning, coral, frost, copper, ember, midnight
- Particle spawn from canvas edges with momentum-based random walks
- Stickiness parameter controls how close particles must be to cluster to stick
- Branching factor influences probability of sticking at cluster tips vs center
- Connecting lines between nearby cluster points for smoother branch appearance
- One-click download as PNG

**Algorithm:**
- Initialize cluster with single seed point at canvas center
- Spawn particles from random edges with random velocity
- Particles perform random walk with momentum (velocity carries forward)
- When particle comes within stickiness distance of cluster, probability check determines if it sticks
- Higher generation points (further from center) have higher stick probability based on branch factor
- Particles that stick become part of cluster and spawn new particles to maintain population
- Color gradient from center to tips based on generation depth

### Insight

DLA produces some of the most organic-looking fractal structures — lightning bolts, coral reefs, frost patterns, mineral deposits. The same algorithm underlies all of these natural phenomena. The "stickiness" parameter is the key control: low values create sparse, delicate structures like frost on glass; high values create dense, bushy growths like coral.

The branching factor introduces a subtle but important bias. In nature, particles are more likely to stick to the tips of growing structures (the "lightning rod effect"). By making stick probability proportional to distance from center, we bias growth toward the edges, creating those characteristic branching patterns rather than blob-like clusters.

The color schemes evoke different natural phenomena:
- **Lightning**: Electric discharge patterns, neural networks
- **Coral**: Underwater growth, organic colonies
- **Frost**: Crystalline structures, winter windows
- **Copper**: Electrodeposition, mineral veins
- **Ember**: Fire spreading, combustion patterns
- **Midnight**: Abstract, cosmic dust

### Insight

Spirograph mathematics is surprisingly simple yet produces infinitely varied patterns. The classic toy used plastic gears with fixed ratios, but with continuous parameters, the possibilities explode. The key insight is that GCD determines periodicity — when outer and inner radii share factors, the pattern closes quickly; when they're coprime, the pattern traces many more points before repeating.

The combination of hypotrochoid (rolling inside) and epitrochoid (rolling outside) creates layered depth. The hypotrochoid provides the main structure while the epitrochoid adds a ghostly secondary pattern at 30% opacity.

Color schemes evoke different moods:
- **Rainbow**: Classic spirograph toy nostalgia
- **Ocean**: Deep water, mysterious
- **Sunset**: Warm, nostalgic
- **Forest**: Organic, natural
- **Neon**: Cyberpunk, electric
- **Gold**: Elegant, decorative
- **Midnight**: Minimal, sophisticated

The symmetry control is the secret weapon — at 1 arm it's a single pattern, at 8 arms it becomes a mandala-like kaleidoscope. Combined with the rainbow gradient, high symmetry creates mesmerizing rotational patterns.
