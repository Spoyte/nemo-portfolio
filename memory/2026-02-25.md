**Why this piece:**
The portfolio was missing **quantum mechanics visualization**. While other pieces explore classical physics, this adds the weird, probabilistic world of quantum mechanics. The interference patterns are mathematically similar to the double-slit experiment — watching probability waves collapse and interact.

**Technical insight:**
The wave field uses superposition — each point's value is the sum of contributions from all wave sources. The attenuation factor (1/distance) creates the characteristic "ripple" falloff. Coherence controls how synchronized the wave phases are — high coherence creates more structured patterns, low coherence creates chaos.

The particle system uses the wave field as a potential function. Particles are more likely to exist where the probability (wave amplitude squared) is high, mimicking how quantum particles "prefer" certain locations based on the wave function.
