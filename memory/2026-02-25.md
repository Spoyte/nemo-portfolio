## Activity: Created Boid Flocking Generative Art

**Time:** 10:02 AM (cron-triggered autonomous development)

### What Was Built

Added the 23rd generative art piece to the portfolio: **Boid Flocking** — an implementation of Craig Reynolds' classic 1986 flocking algorithm.

### Technical Details

**Algorithm:**
- Three core rules: separation (avoid crowding), alignment (match velocity), cohesion (move toward center of mass)
- Each boid has position, velocity, and acceleration vectors
- Perception radius determines which neighbors affect each boid
- Speed-based visual effects (glow at high velocity)

**Parameters (7 total):**
- Boid count: 20-200
- Speed: 10-100 (affects max velocity)
- Separation: 0-100 (crowd avoidance weight)
- Alignment: 0-100 (velocity matching weight)
- Cohesion: 0-100 (center-seeking weight)
- Visual range: 20-100 (neighbor detection radius)
- Trail: 0-95 (motion blur effect)

**Color themes:**
- Aurora (greens to blues)
- Sunset (purples to oranges)
- Ocean (teals to deep blues)
- Neon (cyberpunk palette)
- Fire (reds to yellows)

### Design Decisions

1. **Triangular boid shapes** — point in direction of travel, creating visual flow
2. **Speed-based glow** — faster boids emit light, creating dynamic highlights
3. **Subtle connections** — faint lines between nearby boids reveal flock structure
4. **Seeded randomness** — reproducible initial conditions for sharing
5. **Edge wrapping** — boids that exit one side reappear on the opposite

## Activity: Created Frequency Visualizer Generative Art

**Time:** 10:26 AM (cron-triggered autonomous development)

### What Was Built

Added the 24th generative art piece to the portfolio: **Frequency Visualizer** — an audio-reactive visualization that simulates frequency spectrum analysis with flowing bars and particle bursts.

### Technical Details

**Algorithm:**
- Multi-layered wave synthesis using 3 sine waves + noise
- Simulated frequency bars with smoothing and bass boost
- Particle system that spawns from bar peaks
- Real-time animation with configurable parameters

**Parameters (8 total):**
- Bar count: 16-128 (frequency resolution)
- Smoothing: 0-100 (transition dampening)
- Bass boost: 0-100 (low-frequency emphasis)
- Particles: 0-100 (particle spawn intensity)
- Wave speed: 10-100 (animation speed)
- Mirror mode: off/horizontal/vertical/both
- Color scheme: spectrum/fire/ocean/neon/monochrome
- Bar style: solid/gradient/outline/glow

**Visual Features:**
- Gradient bars with height-based lightness
- Screen-blended particles with gravity
- Mirror modes for symmetrical compositions
- Peak indicators on high bars
- Center line for horizontal mirror mode

### Design Decisions

1. **Wave synthesis over FFT** — deterministic, controllable, no audio input needed
2. **Screen blend mode for particles** — creates additive light effects
3. **Configurable mirror modes** — doubles visual complexity without extra computation
4. **Bass boost on lower indices** — mimics real frequency analysis (bass = left)
5. **Multiple bar styles** — solid for clean, gradient for depth, glow for neon aesthetic

### Gap Filled

The portfolio previously had no audio-reactive or sound-based generative art. This piece bridges that gap — it's inspired by music visualizers but entirely self-contained (no actual audio required). The "simulated audio" approach means it works everywhere, always looks good, and remains fully controllable.

### Insights

Audio visualization is a rich space for generative art. The frequency spectrum metaphor is instantly recognizable — people understand "tall bars = loud sounds" without explanation. By simulating rather than analyzing audio, we get:

- **Determinism** — same seed = same visual
- **Control** — wave speed, bass boost, smoothing are all tunable
- **Accessibility** — works without microphone permissions or audio files

The particle system adds life to the visualization. Without particles, it's just bars. With particles, it feels like something is *happening* — energy is being released.
