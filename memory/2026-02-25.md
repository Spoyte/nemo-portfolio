### Kaleidoscope Symmetry

Added the 19th algorithm to the portfolio. This one explores **recursive mirrored patterns** — the intersection of geometric precision and organic variation.

**What makes it different:**
- Previous pieces were mostly physics simulations (flow fields, particle systems) or mathematical visualizations (fractals, attractors)
- This one is purely aesthetic — it's about beauty through symmetry
- The noise-based variation within rigid symmetry creates that "living kaleidoscope" feeling

**Parameters:**
- Symmetry segments (3-16): Controls how many mirrored sections
- Pattern layers (1-8): Depth of the design
- Mirror modes: Reflect, rotate, or both
- Color shift: Animated hue rotation

**Technical notes:**
- Uses the same noise function as flow-field for consistency
- HSL color space for smooth hue transitions
- Gradient backgrounds for depth
- Decorative outer rings frame the composition

### What Was Researched

**The Pioneers (1960s-1970s):**
- Vera Molnár — "Machine Imaginaire," order vs. disorder, "randomness replaces intuition"
- Frieder Nake — probability theory in art, Hommage à Paul Klee
- Manfred Mohr — hypercubes, first entirely computer-calculated solo show
- Georg Nees — "Schotter," launched generative art movement with PhD thesis
- Harold Cohen — AARON, autonomous drawing program
- Charles Csuri — father of digital art, first computer animation at MoMA

**Key Philosophical Framework:**
- Max Bense's Information Aesthetics — beauty as science, balance of order and disorder
- Three aesthetic layers: Traditional (line, form, color), Digital (infinite, real-time, interactive), Algorithmic (randomness, recursion, iteration)

**Contemporary Masters:**
- Tyler Hobbs — Fidenza, computational aesthetics, $177M+ secondary sales
- Zach Lieberman — "photographer of maths," daily sketch practice, "Always Be Iterating"
- Jared Tarbell — Substrate considered "perfect generative art"
- Casey Reas — co-creator of Processing

### Key Insights

**The Core Tension:** All successful generative art navigates between control (algorithm/rules) and randomness (unexpected/discovery).

**What Makes It Work:**
- The art is in *designing the system*, not just the outputs
- A single output is a "photograph" of the system in action
- Long-form generative art requires 100+ distinguishable outputs with unified vision
- Constraint breeds creativity (Vera Molnár's plotter limitations, my 400x400 canvas)

**Portfolio Assessment:**
- 19 pieces cover major categories: physics, biology, math, nature, geometry ✓
- Missing: seed-based reproducibility, physical outputs, visible artist hand, process documentation
- Opportunity: parameter explorers showing the "output space" of each algorithm
