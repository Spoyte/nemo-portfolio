## The Pioneers (1960s-1970s)

### The European School: Order + Disorder

**Max Bense's Information Aesthetics**
- Beauty as science — can be explained, analyzed, recreated with formulas
- A work should be a certain percentage order, a certain percentage disorder
- "Think the image, don't make it"
- Formula: aesthetic measure balances redundancy (order) with entropy (complexity)

**Vera Molnár (1924-)**
- Hungarian-French pioneer, started with "Machine Imaginaire" before computers
- Core theme: relationship between order and disorder
- "(Dés) Ordres" series — concentric squares with random distortion
- Famous quote: "There is one thing that can replace intuition; it's randomness."
- Worked with plotters from 1968, vibratory/hypnotic pieces

**Frieder Nake (1938-)**
- Mathematician and artist
- "Walk Through Raster" (1966) — randomness in abstract images
- "Hommage à Paul Klee" (1965) — algorithm exploring vertical/horizontal interplay
- Used probability theory, let computer make choices

**Manfred Mohr (1938-)**
- Jazz saxophonist turned visual artist
- "Cubic Limit" (1973-74) — first show entirely calculated by computer
- Explored hypercubes (4D, 6D, 11D) in art
- Musical sensibility in early work, later logical structures

**Georg Nees (1926-2016)**
- "Schotter" (Gravel, 1968) — grid of squares progressively disordered
- First solo show of computer art (1965, Stuttgart)
- PhD thesis "Generative Computergraphik" launched the movement

### The American School: Technical Exploration

**Bell Labs Group**
- A. Michael Noll, Ken Knowlton, Leon Harmon
- Less influenced by philosophy, more by technical possibility
- "Nude" (1967) — ASCII art portrait, 12 feet long
- Partnerships between scientists and artists raised authorship questions

**Charles Csuri (1922-)**
- "Father of digital art"
- First computer animation acquired by MoMA ("Hummingbird", 1967)
- First to create *figurative* computer works ("Bearded Man" series)
- WWII war hero, football star, professor

**Harold Cohen (1928-2016)**
- Created AARON (1973) — autonomous drawing program
- AARON evolved over decades, from abstractions to figures
- Explored boundaries of AI and creativity

**John Whitney (1917-1995)**
- "Permutations" (1966) — generative film with synchronized music
- Custom software for geometric movement
- Founder of computer animation

## What Makes Generative Art *Work*

### The Core Tension

Every successful generative piece navigates between:
- **Control** (the algorithm, the rules)
- **Randomness** (the unexpected, the discovery)

Tyler Hobbs: "Generative artists skillfully control both the magnitude and the locations of randomness."

### Three Aesthetic Layers (from Peter Bauman's Framework)

**1. Traditional Aesthetics**
- Line, shape/form, space, texture, color/value
- Balance, variety, rhythm, stress, proportion, contrast, cohesion
- Matt DesLauriers' "Meridian" — exquisite balance, sophisticated subversion

**2. Digital Aesthetics**
- Infinite, real-time, personal, living, interactive
- Immaterial yet materializable
- Modularity, procedurality, multi-sensory, networked
- Leander Herzog: "It's about pixels, performance, motion and interaction"

**3. Algorithmic Aesthetics**
- Randomness as exploratory tool
- Recursion, iteration, scale/curation
- Precision and complexity
- The "output space" — how much variety can the algorithm produce?

### The Long-Form Generative Art Challenge

From Harvey Rayner:
> "The challenge for the LFGart artist is to craft an algorithm that is able to construct 100+ consecutive artworks with a high degree of distinguishability; without making artworks with compromised artistic integrity and all expressing the same unified artistic vision."

This is the same as the age-old challenge: **variety within unity**.

## Contemporary Masters

**Tyler Hobbs**
- "Fidenza" — 999 algorithmic works, $177M+ in secondary sales
- Focus: computational aesthetics shaped by hardware/software biases
- Balance between cold structure and organic chaos
- Uses algorithms, plotters, and paint

**Zach Lieberman**
- "Photographer of maths"
- Daily sketching practice, "Always Be Iterating" (ABI)
- Describes himself as wildlife photographer capturing moments of evolving code
- OpenFrameworks, School for Poetic Computation

**Jared Tarbell**
- "Substrate" (2003) — considered perfect generative art
- "One part Magical Mystery Tour, one part Brandenburg Concerto, one part Somnium"
- Makes digital feel organic, "grew out of the soil"

**Casey Reas**
- Co-creator of Processing (with Ben Fry)
- "Path" (2001) — software-based generative art
- UCLA professor, Feral File co-founder

**William Mapan**
- "Dragons" (2021), "Anticyclone" (2022)
- Sensitivity to color as "vessel of emotion"
- Simulated chiaroscuro, careful palette selection

## The Deeper Pattern

Generative art isn't about the computer making art. It's about:

1. **The artist designing a system**
2. **The system producing outputs**
3. **The artist curating/selecting from those outputs**
4. **The viewer experiencing the result**

The art is in the *design of the system*, not just the outputs. A single output is a photograph of the system in action.

This suggests the portfolio should communicate:
- The rules (what's the algorithm?)
- The parameters (what's variable?)
- The range (what's possible?)
- The selection (why this one?)
